# Free feeds cover avoidance; paid sources still win the first minute

Free and official sources can cover three of the four needs for VAN/VATI: official primary releases, an economic-calendar schedule, and historical archives. They cannot reliably cover the fourth, real-time market-moving headlines and timely actuals, closely enough to trade the first move after a release. On 2026-10-15, keyless official RSS/API endpoints returned live, same-day items for the Fed, ECB, BoE, BoJ, BoC, RBA, RBNZ, SNB, ONS, Eurostat, StatCan, ABS and SEC EDGAR. The free ForexFactory/Faireconomy export and the MetaTrader 5 built-in calendar between them supply schedules, forecasts, actuals and revised-previous values. ALFRED, GDELT, Common Crawl news, Dukascopy and TrueFX supply a free event-study backbone. The weak points are consistent. **No free source reached in this research publishes a documented survey consensus with revision history.** **Free headline feeds lag paid squawks by seconds to minutes.** **Practitioners report that MT5 calendar actuals arrive about 1 to 5+ minutes late.** Zimbabwe is the hardest category: **RBZ, ZIMSTAT, ZSE and VFEX publish no RSS or public API** (SECZim's WordPress feed is the one exception), and ZSE/VFEX exchange data is licensed only through the paid ZSE Data Direct portal, which publishes no price. The design rule of two independent sources per category, with VERIFIED only when two sources agree, can be met for G10 macro actuals and schedules using free sources. It can be met only in a weakened form for consensus and for Zimbabwe. It cannot be met for sub-minute headlines without at least one paid feed. The cheapest credible paid additions seen on 2026-10-15 are EODHD Fundamentals at $59.99/mo for a calendar API with actual/estimate fields, FinancialJuice paid tiers (about $23/mo, a third-party figure) or Newsquawk Basic at $199/mo for fast headlines, and an enquiry-priced ZSE Data Direct subscription.

**Evidence status and limits.** All prices and availability checks are as seen on **2026-10-15**. Items labelled "verified" were confirmed from the provider's own page or by a live HTTP probe that day. Items labelled "third-party" or "unverified" were not. **Reddit (r/algotrading, r/Forex, r/Daytrading, r/quant) and BabyPips could not be retrieved.** Reddit endpoints returned 403/429, PullPush refused agent scraping, and BabyPips returned a Cloudflare challenge that was not bypassed. Practitioner evidence therefore comes from Elite Trader, Hacker News, the MQL5 forum, QuantConnect, GitHub issues, and search-engine summaries of ForexFactory and NexusFi threads. Terms-of-service texts for ForexFactory, Investing.com, Trading Economics, investingLive, Dukascopy and the Zimbabwean sites were **not read**. Every ToS judgement below is therefore a risk assessment, not a legal reading.

## Official primary sources: thirteen live feeds, but Zimbabwe has almost none

The G10 official layer is in better shape than its reputation suggests. Live probes on 2026-10-15 returned HTTP 200 feeds with items from that day or the day before. **Bank of England** news RSS had a newest item at 10:52 on 15 Oct 2026 ([BoE RSS](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/rss/news)). The other live sources were the **Bank of Japan** What's New RSS ([BoJ](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/rss/whatsnew.xml)), **RBA** media releases ([RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/rss/rss-cb-media-releases.xml)), **RBNZ** news ([RBNZ](https://www.rbnz.govt.nz/feeds/news)), **SNB** news ([SNB](https://www.snb.ch/public/rss/en/news)), **ECB** press RSS ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/rss/press.html)), the keyless **Bank of Canada Valet** API and press feed ([BoC Valet](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/valet/observations/FXUSDCAD/json?recent=2)), and the keyless **ECB Data API** at `data-api.ecb.europa.eu/service/` ([ECB Data API](https://data-api.ecb.europa.eu/service/data/EXR/D.USD.EUR.SP00.A?lastNObservations=1&format=jsondata)). The Federal Reserve publishes per-category press feeds, including `press_monetary.xml`, per-official speech feeds and H.15/H.10 release feeds. It has **no FOMC calendar feed**, so meeting dates must be taken from its HTML calendar ([Fed RSS](https://www.federalreserve.gov/feeds/feeds.htm)). SARB's RSS page links a Solr feed URL that was not fetched, so SARB is unverified ([SARB](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/quick-links/rss-feeds)). The feeds mix RSS 2.0, RSS 1.0/RDF (BoC, RBA) and the RSS-CB namespace (SNB), so the ingest parser must handle all of them.

Statistics agencies supply actual values, but almost none supply schedules in machine-readable form. Keyless APIs were confirmed for **Eurostat** (JSON-stat and SDMX), **StatCan WDS**, **ABS SDMX** and the **ONS beta API** at `api.beta.ons.gov.uk`. **BEA** needs a free registered UserID ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/prc_hicp_manr?geo=EA&lastTimePeriod=1&coicop=CP00); [StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/wds/rest/getAllCubesListLite); [ABS](https://data.api.abs.gov.au/rest/dataflow/all?detail=allstubs); [BEA](https://apps.bea.gov/api/data?&UserID=x&method=GETDATASETLIST)). Only **ONS** offered a working release-calendar RSS ([ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/releasecalendar?rss)). The Eurostat and ABS calendars are HTML only. One Eurostat HICP query returned an `updated` stamp of 2026-02-06, which is unexplained and should be checked before relying on that endpoint.

The **BLS** is a cautionary case. Its v2 API allows 500 queries/day and 50 requests per 10 seconds, and returns "Database is locked" around releases ([BLS FAQ](https://www.bls.gov/developers/api_faqs.htm)). Its site crashed on jobs days in 2011 ([Nextgov](https://nextgov.com/modernization/2011/12/employment-announcement-crashes-bls-website/232481)). It inadvertently posted CPI files about 30 minutes early in May 2024 ([BLS notice](https://www.BLS.GOV/bls/early-data-release-05152024.htm)). Two design rules follow from this. First, poll release pages rather than the BLS API at release time. Second, reject any "actual" whose timestamp precedes the scheduled release.

For commodities, the **EIA** posts WPSR CSV tables at 10:30 ET on Wednesdays, with holiday shifts to Thursday ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/schedule.php)). The **CFTC** publishes COT at 3:30 pm ET on Fridays ([CFTC](https://cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ReleaseSchedule/index.htm)). **SEC EDGAR** allows 10 requests/second with a declared User-Agent ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data)). Its latest-filings Atom feed answered live ([EDGAR Atom](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcurrent&type=8-K&count=10&output=atom)), while true real-time PDS dissemination is fee-based.

Zimbabwe breaks this pattern. The **RBZ** publishes daily ZWG Bid/Ask/Avg rates for USD, GBP, EUR, ZAR and BWP as Joomla HTML, and MPC/MPS statements as PDFs. Its 2026 MPC dates were listed as 27 Mar, 29 Jun and 28 Sep, plus 17 Nov at 10:00, which appears to be a future meeting. **No RSS, API or spreadsheet download was found** ([RBZ](https://www.rbz.co.zw)). **ZIMSTAT** posts monthly CPI as web pages; the July 2026 release showed ZWG y/y 3.2% and USD y/y 3.1%. No forward release calendar was found ([ZIMSTAT CPI](https://zimstat.co.zw/consumer-price-index/); [July 2026](https://zimstat.co.zw/july-2026-price-statistics-release/)). **SECZim** has a working WordPress feed, but its newest items were vacancy notices, so it needs filtering ([SECZim feed](https://www.seczim.co.zw/feed/)). The **ZSE and VFEX** sites are JavaScript-rendered Next.js apps with no price links in static HTML, and some ZSE fetches returned HTTP 405 ([ZSE](https://www.zse.co.zw/); [VFEX](https://www.vfex.exchange/)). The compliant machine route is **ZSE Data Direct**, launched May 2025. It covers ZSE and VFEX end-of-day reports, history and a view-only live terminal in 3-, 6- or 12-month packages. **Its price is not published** and no programmatic API documentation was found ([African Markets](https://african-markets.com/en/stock-markets/zse/zimbabwe-stock-exchange-announces-launch-of-market-data-platform-data-direct); [GlobalExchanges](https://globalexchanges.com/latest-news/zimbabwe-zse-announces-launch-of-market-data-platform-data-direct/141895/)). The aggregator Mutapa Times states that "end-of-day pricing is the only public feed available" and that a complete daily list is a paid Data Direct product ([Mutapa Times](https://mutapatimes.com/markets)). Third-party scrapers such as Apify Mansa Labs (from $5 per 1,000 results), Afristox and a GitHub/RapidAPI price-sheet scraper all have unclear licensing ([Apify](https://apify.com/mansalabs/african-stock-market-data); [Afristox](https://www.afristox.com/api); [GitHub](https://github.com/bevennyamande/Zimbabwe-Stock-Exchange-Daily-Pricesheets)). All three Ministry of Finance domains failed to connect from the research environment. Bloomberg/LSEG coverage of ZSE/VFEX is unverified. **Intraday automated ZSE/VFEX trading off a licensed data API does not appear to be available off the shelf.**

## The calendar splits into schedule, consensus and actuals

No free source carries all four fields the surprise scorer needs (time, consensus, actual, revision) at release-time latency. The **ForexFactory/Faireconomy weekly export** was live on 2026-10-15. Its fields are `title, country, date, impact, forecast, previous`, with ISO-8601 New York-offset times, and it has **no `actual` field** ([FF JSON](https://nfs.faireconomy.media/ff_calendar_thisweek.json)). Practitioners report a per-IP limit of 2 downloads per 5 minutes since August 2024 ("subject to change"), an hourly update cycle, and an HTML "Request Denied" page returned when the limit is exceeded. Their advice is to poll every 30–60 minutes and share one file per VPS ([FF thread](https://www.forexfactory.com/thread/1311021-mql45-programmers-this-weekly-news-download-code-solves), via search summary). One GitHub PR shows the defensive pattern: detect HTML before JSON-parsing, and cache for an hour ([Quantumtraders PR #31](https://github.com/emcaballero94-wq/Quantumtraders/pull/31)).

The **MT5 built-in calendar** is the richest free source. `MqlCalendarValue` carries `actual_value`, `forecast_value`, `prev_value` and `revised_prev_value` (scaled ×10⁶), and the documentation example requests history from 2018 ([MQL5 docs](https://www.mql5.com/en/docs/calendar/calendarvaluehistory)). It has four practical defects. First, it is **reachable only from MQL5**: the official Python package exposes no calendar, so a read-only EA has to export CSV, with a refresh floor of 30 seconds ([CodeBase 76951](https://www.mql5.com/en/code/76951)). Second, it **returns nothing in the Strategy Tester** ([MQL5 forum](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/326554/14553609)). Third, it **stores only the last state of each entry**, so forecast-change history is lost ([MQL5 book](https://www.mql5.com/de/book/advanced/calendar/calendar_change_last)). Fourth, users have repeatedly reported **late actuals**: 2 min 43 s on NFP in 2019, about 1 minute on US CPI in 2025, and "delays up to hours" plus vanished UK events in February 2026. A moderator noted that the calendar "is uploaded by the robot" ([MQL5 #317370](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/317370); [MQL5 #505494](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/505494)). MetaQuotes does not document whether `forecast_value` is a survey consensus.

The other free routes are weaker. **cTrader** refuses calendar access to cBots because the data is licensed "to display ... to the end user" (a 2021 Spotware statement; [cTrader forum](https://community.ctrader.com/forum/ctrader-algo/35423/)). **Investing.com** sits behind Cloudflare. A GitHub issue roughly three weeks before 2026-10-15 reports 403s and challenges on both Node and Go clients, with no durable bypass ([yahoo-stock-mcp #113](https://github.com/jamespud/yahoo-stock-mcp/issues/113)). An MQL5 moderator observed that calendar sites change URL formats, apparently to stop EA use ([MQL5 #488168](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/488168)). The **Trading Economics** `guest:guest` key reportedly returns only a fixed demo set or no longer works (both third-party reports; [api-evangelist](https://github.com/api-evangelist/tradingeconomics); [hex package](https://preview.hex.pm/preview/trading_economics/0.1.0)).

Paid calendar APIs fill the consensus gap at a range of prices. **Trading Economics** is the best documented. It separates `Forecast` (survey consensus, "the average forecast among a representative group of economists") from its own `TEForecast` and has a `Revised` field ([TE docs](https://docs.tradingeconomics.com/economic_calendar/snapshot/)). Third-party figures put it at about $149–$299/mo billed yearly; the official matrix needs a login. **EODHD** is the cheapest verified API with actuals. Its Economic Events endpoint returns `actual, previous, estimate, change`, with history "from 2020", on the **Fundamentals plan at $59.99/mo** or All-In-One at $99.99/mo. Its `estimate` is defined only as a "forecast" from an unnamed source ([EODHD pricing](https://eodhd.com/pricing); [EODHD docs](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis/economic-events-data-api)). **FMP** listed Starter at $19/mo billed annually ($29 monthly) on its official page. Whether that tier includes the "Economic Data Releases Calendar" row is **unverified** ([FMP pricing](https://site.financialmodelingprep.com/pricing-plans)). Finnhub's pricing page is JavaScript-only, and third-party figures conflict ($11.99–$200/mo), so its calendar tier is unverified.

Practitioners reached by this research mostly use these sources to avoid news, not to trade it. EA guidance clusters around blackouts of 15–30 minutes before and after high-impact events ([MQL5 blog](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/773936); [mt4copier](https://www.mt4copier.com/news-filter-copier/)). It warns of 2–3 hour broker-server timezone offsets and of stop slippage that filters do not prevent. On Elite Trader in July 2026, the answers to "best free economic calendar" were Trading Economics and Myfxbook ([ET 390430](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/trading-and-economic-calendar.390430/)). QuantConnect staff point users to EODHD/TE datasets or to downloading ForexFactory ([QC 18280](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/18280/is-there-a-way-to-read-the-event-calenders-like-forexfactory-calender/)).

## Free headlines are seconds to minutes late, and fast X reads cost money

Every free headline route reached in this research is either polled, delayed or licence-restricted. **investingLive** (ForexLive rebranded on 21 July 2025) served a live RSS 2.0 breaking-news feed on 2026-10-15; `forexlive.com/feed/news` redirects to `investinglive.com/feed/news` ([investingLive RSS](https://investinglive.com/feed/news); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/forexlive-is-becoming-investinglive-on-july-21/)). Its terms on automated use were not extracted. **Reuters has had no official public RSS since June 2020** ([Feeder KB](https://feeder.co/knowledge-base/rss-content/reuters-rss-feeds/)). The workaround of a Google News `allinurl:reuters.com` RSS query adds indexing delay ([Codarium](https://codarium.substack.com/p/returning-the-killed-rss-of-reuters)), and FiveFilters-generated Reuters feeds broke when the site moved to JavaScript ([FiveFilters](https://forum.fivefilters.org/t/reuters-rss-feeds-are-down/1152)).

Free news APIs are too rate-limited to act as triggers. Marketaux allows 100 requests/day, at 3 articles each ([Marketaux](https://marketaux.com/pricing)). Alpha Vantage allows 25/day (third-party figure). NewsAPI.org's free tier is development-only with a 24-hour delay ([NewsAPI](https://newsapi.org/pricing)). EODHD's free tier allows 20 calls/day, and each news request costs 5 calls. Tiingo's free tier is 1,000 requests/day under an internal-use licence, but whether it includes news is unverified ([Tiingo](https://www.tiingo.com/about/pricing)). Finnhub's free REST tier (about 60 calls/min, third-party figure) is the only one that allows polling roughly every second. Its "personal use" terms and the free availability of its news WebSocket are unverified.

The fast tier is paid. **X removed free API reads for new developers on 2026-02-06** (third-party). X's own page lists reads at **$0.005 per post**, capped at **3M reads per month**; third-party guides say 2M ([X pricing](https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing)). Polling a handful of squawk accounts is estimated at about $150–250/mo; this is an estimate, not a measured cost. **FinancialJuice** is the free benchmark practitioners cite. In June 2026 an Elite Trader poster saw a headline on FinancialJuice before a rival free feed ([ET 390116](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/made-a-free-tool-to-see-news-events-that-move-the-market-with-upcoming-high-impact-events.390116/)). Its free tier is delayed, by about 10 s per older reports and 15 s or more per a competitor ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/showthread.php?t=39144&page=2); [Newsquawk compare](https://www.newsquawk.com/compare/newsquawk-vs-financialjuice)). Its terms reportedly license personal use only. Paid FinancialJuice is about $23/mo (third-party, unverified). **Newsquawk Basic is $199/mo** (official page; [Newsquawk](https://newsquawk.com/pricing)). **LiveSquawk is £275/$350 per person per month** ([LiveSquawk](https://livesquawk.com)). **GDELT** updates every 15 minutes, and one blogger reported 85–100% request refusals on its DOC API ([jwatte](https://jwatte.com/blog/gdelt-open-data-kalev-leetaru/)), so it is context, not a trigger. One HN user reads @DeItaone free through the xcancel.com front-end ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46465043)). That is a ToS-grey, fragile route and should not feed an automated system.

The only dated first-hand latency figures found came from Elite Trader. A free scraped feed logged jobless claims at 08:30:29 and housing starts at 08:30:50 in November 2023 ([ET 377074](https://www.elitetrader.com/et/threads/i-created-a-free-news-feed-site-with-major-impact-events.377074/)), and logged CPI 9 seconds after release in June 2026. A scalping guide reports spreads of 5–10x normal within seconds on NFP ([mt4programming](https://mt4programming.com/beyond-the-backtest-how-to-stop-mt4-spread-expansion-from-killing-your-scalping-ea/)). Together these mean free feeds are fine for blackout logic and post-release regime filters, and inadequate for first-tick reaction trading.

## Free archives support event studies, except for consensus

The **FRED/ALFRED API** with `output_type=4` returns US values "as first released", and `realtime_start` gives the release date ([FRED API](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/series_observations.html); [ALFRED](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/help/downloaddata)). It requires an attribution notice and sets no fixed rate limit ([FRED ToU](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/terms_of_use.html)). The **Philadelphia Fed RTDSM** adds first, second and third release files ([RTDSM](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/real-time-data-set-for-macroeconomists)). Neither has intraday timestamps, so minute-precise release times must come from calendars.

Free consensus history exists only as unlicensed community scrapes. Examples are a Kaggle ForexFactory dump of 81,769 rows to 2024-08-16 with a revised-previous flag ([Baselight/Kaggle](https://baselight.app/u/kaggle/dataset/randelltsen_forex_factory_entire_dataset_till_2024_08_16)) and a Hugging Face CSV covering 2007-01 to 2025-04 with unclear provenance ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Tropstan/Forex_Factory_Calendar/blob/main/README.md)). These are research-only, and re-uploading data does not transfer rights. The academic standard (Andersen–Bollerslev–Diebold–Vega 2003) used proprietary MMS survey medians, 5-minute bid/ask-interpolated FX, and surprises standardized as (A−E)/σ ([NBER w8959](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w8959/w8959.pdf)). The same standardization gives VAN/VATI its "how big versus history" score.

News archives are **GDELT 2.0** (15-minute batches since February 2015) and **Common Crawl CC-NEWS** (since August 2016, with a bias toward outlets that publish RSS) ([ONS GDELT appendix](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/birthsdeathsandmarriages/deaths/methodologies/globaldatabaseofeventslanguageandtonegdeltappendix); [Common Crawl](https://blog.commoncrawl.org/blog/news-dataset-available)). Both suit 30m/4h windows, not 1-minute attribution. For prices, **Dukascopy** offers free tick/M1 data on 1,600+ instruments, including metals, oil and index CFDs, under its own usage agreement, which was not read; it is anecdotally "full of holes" ([dukascopy lib](https://github.com/theorycraft-trading/dukascopy); [MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/forum/138020)). **TrueFX** offers millisecond top-of-book data since 2009 with a free account ([TFX](https://rdrr.io/cran/TFX)). **HistData.com** offers M1 data with an unknown upstream source. **Massive (ex-Polygon)** forex minute files go back to 2009-09-25, at **$49/mo** ([Massive](https://massive.com/docs/flat-files/forex/minute-aggregates/2025/07)). For CFDs, the trading broker's own MT5 history is the most execution-relevant series.

## Recommended free-first stack and where two-source verification breaks

The table applies the design rule of two independent sources per category. It separates **independent origin** (two different producers of the number) from **independent retrieval** (two pipes carrying the same origin's number). For actuals, the official agency is the origin, so "VERIFIED" in practice means the agency figure plus one calendar's transcription agreeing. That catches transcription and latency errors, not a mis-release. For consensus, two sources legitimately disagree because they sample different economists. An exact-match VERIFIED rule will rarely fire on consensus, so the recorder should either accept a tolerance band or label consensus as CORROBORATED or SINGLE-SOURCE. This is a design recommendation inferred from the evidence, not something tested.

| Category | Free source A (verified live/official 2026-10-15) | Free source B / cross-check | Independence quality | Gap needing paid source | Cheapest credible paid option (seen 2026-10-15) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scheduled times | Official calendars: ONS RSS, Fed FOMC page, RBZ MPC listing, EIA/CFTC schedules | FF weekly export (hourly, ≤2 pulls/5 min) and MT5 calendar | Strong (different origins) | None for G10. ZIMSTAT has no calendar | n/a |
| Actuals | Official APIs: ECB, Eurostat, ONS beta, StatCan, ABS, BEA (free key), EIA CSV, ALFRED | MT5 calendar actuals (1–5+ min late, per users) | Same origin, independent retrieval | Release-tick speed | EODHD Fundamentals $59.99/mo (verified); TE about $149+/mo (third-party) |
| Consensus | FF export `forecast` (methodology undocumented) | MT5 `forecast_value` (provenance undocumented) | Weak (neither documents a survey) | Documented survey consensus and revision history | TE (`Forecast` vs `TEForecast`, `Revised`); EODHD `estimate` (source unnamed) |
| Revisions | MT5 `revised_prev_value`; ALFRED vintages (US) | Official agency revised prints | Strong for US, partial elsewhere | Forecast-change history (MT5 keeps last state only) | TE `Revised` |
| G10 headlines | Central-bank RSS ×8 plus EDGAR Atom (official) | investingLive RSS; Finnhub free REST (ToS unverified) | Strong origin diversity, but polled | Sub-10 s market headlines | FinancialJuice paid (about $23/mo, third-party, personal licence); Newsquawk Basic $199/mo; X API $0.005/read |
| Zimbabwe macro/policy | RBZ HTML rates and MPC PDFs; ZIMSTAT CPI pages; SECZim RSS | Herald, NewsDay, Financial Gazette echoes | Same origin, independent retrieval | Machine-readable feeds of any kind | None found. Scrape politely, after reading robots.txt and terms |
| ZSE/VFEX prices and announcements | African Financials documents; ZSE/VFEX sites (JS, bot-hostile) | African Markets / Mutapa Times EOD | Unclear licensing | Licensed EOD data and any intraday data | ZSE Data Direct (price on enquiry to data@zse.co.zw) |

The terms-of-service risk ranks as follows. **Lowest** are the official government and central-bank feeds: public-domain or open, with rate limits and FRED's attribution rule. **Moderate** are investingLive, Finnhub, Tiingo, FMP (which requires free-tier attribution, [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40396712)) and FinancialJuice, all personal or internal-use licences. **High** are ForexFactory and Investing.com scraping, Apify resale scrapers, xcancel/Nitter mirrors and ZSE scrapers. The enforcement reported by practitioners was technical (IP blocks and Cloudflare challenges); no bans or legal actions against individual scrapers were found. One more item is relevant if Reddit were ever a planned input: Wikipedia states that Reddit announced closing its public API and RSS in November 2026. This was **not independently verified** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit_API_controversy)).

## Conclusion

The research shifts the design question from whether free data is reliable to which job each source is fit for. Free official feeds are the most trustworthy origin for actuals and policy text, more so than any aggregator. But they are polled, sometimes locked at release, and occasionally early, so the recorder should timestamp-gate them against the official schedule rather than trust arrival order. Consensus is the hidden single point of failure. Every free "forecast" has undocumented provenance, so the surprise scorer's denominator rests on the least verifiable number in the pipeline. The highest-value first paid purchase is therefore a documented consensus with revisions (TE, or EODHD as the cheaper and less documented option), not faster headlines. Faster headlines matter only if VAN/VATI moves from blackout logic to reacting in the first minute, which practitioners in 2026 increasingly describe as an execution problem rather than a data problem.

For Zimbabwe, the two-source rule cannot be met with independent origins at all. Each number has one issuer (RBZ, ZIMSTAT or ZSE), and the media only echoes it. The honest implementation is to record these values as SINGLE-ORIGIN, cross-checked only for transcription, and to treat a ZSE Data Direct enquiry as a prerequisite for any compliant automated ZSE/VFEX trading rather than an optional upgrade. Three cheap probes would settle the most consequential unknowns. The first is to log MT5 calendar actual-arrival times against agency release times for a month. The second is to confirm FMP Starter's calendar coverage and Finnhub's free-tier terms in a browser. The third is to request Data Direct pricing and licence terms. Each probe could reverse a stack choice above.
