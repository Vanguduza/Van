0. Contract, scope and non-claims

0.1 Positive product outcome

VAN Next is one Android application with two photometrically and structurally consistent themes: warm silver/ivory and deep graphite. It feels machined, precisely assembled, softly elevated and high quality. One primary intent is visually dominant per screen. Its state and action system is live and accountable. The app makes complexity available in context, not permanently visible on Home. It is an AI command centre, professional trading workstation, project/agent control plane, personal assistant, automation portal and knowledge environment, not only a trading dashboard.

0.2 Requirements that cannot be thinned out

  1. Both user-approved designs are visually locked; the only sanctioned changes are those explicitly approved by the owner or demanded by essential accessibility/operational correctness, followed by visual re-approval.
  2. The orb is a realistic, sculpted metallic object; animation and state colour are subtle, semantically grounded and informative. No homepage state-key/legend.
  3. One tap on the Home orb opens a deliberately compact read-only summary and navigation sheet; position performance, priority tasks, project progress, notifications and quick destinations appear as concise rows. It must not become a dashboard within a popup.
  4. The global Trading Home visibly provides Markets, Watchlist, Potential trades, Chart and Intelligence entry points and a restrained equity/portfolio-heat context. The locked images show three of the five tiles; Chart and Intelligence tiles are an owner-requested addition (OD-03, visual delta VD-02), so the native golden includes them and this is not drift.
  5. Dedicated Trading screens preserve professional live charts, indicators, position lines, SL/TP, depth where supported, history, orders and safe control paths; not all data is crammed onto one portrait page.
  6. Intelligence has substantive News and News Analysis (OD-11): several independent live sources, cross-checked; macro/event context, provenance, contradicting evidence, affected instruments, relevant strategies/positions, timing and freshness; and comparison with how similar past events actually moved the market, which feeds real trading decisions through defined, tested paths (§8). A live news trading bundle lets VAN and the owner trade news as it breaks, within seconds, through pre-validated event strategies and armed plans (§8.10).
  7. Projects/Tasks offer live spatial diagrams of phases, tasks, dependencies, agents, handoffs, blocked edges, evidence, completed work and current progress; a list alternative remains available.
  8. Floating VAN retains meaningful gestures, modes, dialogue, voice, workboards, dock/minimise/recovery, contextual controls and system-wide usefulness. Re-skinning must not regress these capabilities.
  9. Earlier blueprints are evidence and starting hypotheses, not immutable implementation strategies. Improvements found through validated research, including REA-assisted MT5/TradingView study within the qualified, owner-authorized research scope, can revise this blueprint. Trading and owner-authority changes still require their proper separate domain approval/evidence.
  10. Architecture and product acceptance are different from implementation and production qualification. Never treat mockups, source-only tests, historical branch claims or this blueprint as a deployed result.
  11. Owner manual trading (OD-01, OD-07…OD-10): the owner can open, add to and close trades manually. Manual entries are governed by the account's signed risk profile (mandate, per-strategy budget and, once admitted, trading temperament), always carry a strict, automated, broker-side stop-loss attached inside the opening order, and take an optional take-profit. Adding to a winning manual trade works like a new entry. A manual trade can be handed to VAN for automatic management and taken back. The owner can add to a winning automatic trade, governed by that trade's own risk profile. Manual exits reduce risk and stay available when new entries are blocked. Manual trading is a new origin into the existing VATI path, never a second execution path (§7.4). VAN also reviews the owner's trading habits and turns proven winning patterns into candidate strategies for normal validation (§7.4.12–§7.4.13).
  12. Consolidate before rebuilding (OD-05): functions that exist on unmerged branches or are documented for porting are consolidated and inventoried before the new frontend replaces anything, so the rebuild cannot silently thin the product. The new frontend is built so legacy presentation code cannot leak into it (§12.5–§12.6).

0.3 Explicit exclusions

  • No trading-policy amendment, broker activation, production order, live fund movement or privileged bypass is authorised by this design document. Manual trading (§7.4) needs its own owner-signed mandate version before any live use; the design does not grant it.
  • No automatic merge of PR #93/#95/#97/#92/#70/#72/#77 or deletion of legacy source on the basis of visual preferences alone.
  • REA was separately authorized and its static studies exist. Runtime investigation remains source/version-specific and never grants broker or production authority. Do not repeat the obsolete “host not yet approved for REA” blocker.
  • No VPN, WireGuard, firewall, OCI/IAM, Cloudflare tunnel, host credentials, device pairing or release-signing changes in the frontend programme without distinct operational authorisation and evidence.
  • No invented current balances, prices, performance, task counts, agent states, news, subscriptions, sources or completion percentages in shipping screens.
  • No new standalone page merely because a capability exists. Preserve sheet, modal, inline, activity and intake semantics where those are safer or more coherent.

2. Owner visual authority and anti-drift policy

2.1 Source hierarchy

  1. Recorded owner decisions (OWNER_DECISIONS_AND_CONSOLIDATION.md) and the locked screenshots, as relabelled under §2.6, govern visual style, feature additions and product priorities. A chat transcript is not a durable authority; a decision that is not in the record is not yet binding on development packets.   2. Real operating security/financial/permission authority and source-backed operational truth remain binding functional constraints; a design document cannot silently mint execution authority.
  3. Current immutable repository code, genuine runtime receipts, qualified existing services, current project/branch state and exact Android device evidence inform functional reuse and migration.
  4. Current proven behavior and useful historic user flows are preservation candidates only after admission into a reuse register.
  5. Older design/terminal/visual blueprints, historical audits and unmerged candidates are informative hypotheses and may be corrected, replaced or superseded after evidence-backed comparison.
  6. Generic industry references are inspiration only; they cannot displace the locked designs or introduce unqualified execution semantics.

2.2 What is visually locked

Theme identity: same manufactured metallic orb, same sculpted material grammar, quiet lighting, calm hierarchy, negative space and professional labels. Dark is deep blue/graphite with subtle chrome and restrained soft-cyan glints; light is satin silver/off-white with charcoal readable typography, shallow natural shadows and icy-blue select accents. Components are physically dimensional, not flat Material rectangles and not neon-bordered dashboard cards.

Composition: Home orients around the orb and only a few concise previews; Trading Home is a compact financial overview and five capability entrances (three drawn in the locked art, two owner-added: VD-02); the instrument chart owns the space on the chart screen; position lists remain readable; Intelligence uses discrete actionable opportunities and research/News entry; Work/Projects organise agents and tasks through focused modules and interactive flow views. Micro-detail lives in next-level sheets and screens.

Motion: subtle rotation/glide/rim illumination and physically plausible shadow changes; gentle card rise/press depth; controlled spatial transitions. No constant full-screen bloom, random orbs, gratuitous particles, scroll-jacking, perpetual pulse for every card, or event-colour legend on Home.

7.4.3 Strict automated stop-loss, attached inside the order

  • The system sets the stop. A versioned, deterministic ManualStopPolicy computes it from the instrument's volatility on the chosen timeframe (an ATR multiple per instrument class), floored at the venue's min_stop_distance. The owner sees it before confirming; there is no "no stop" option.
  • The stop goes in the same request as the order. The OrderCommand carries protective_stop (and targets for a TP), and the adapter sends them together: MT5 sl/tp fields; cTrader relativeStopLoss/relativeTakeProfit for market orders and stopLoss/takeProfit for limit orders. The broker therefore opens the position with its stop, or rejects the whole order; the account is never holding a manual position that is waiting for a stop. Pending manual orders carry their stop and TP from the moment they rest at the broker.
  • Invalid submissions are reduced by pre-send validation; broker rejection remains possible. The preview checks the stop against the venue's live stop level, freeze level and current spread, to reject a known-invalid stop before transmission; a changed broker condition can still cause refusal. If the market moves during review, the sheet re-prices instead of sending a stop the broker would refuse.
  • If the broker's reply does not confirm the stop (a rare transport or reporting gap, not the normal path), Rev 1.2 changes the fallback from "close immediately" to: (1) re-read the position from the broker, since confirmation can lag the fill report; (2) if the stop is genuinely missing, re-attach the same stop with modify_stop, up to two attempts within a few seconds; (3) only if the position is still unprotected after that, close it and alert. Each step is recorded. This avoids paying spread and commission to close and reopen a position whose stop was merely reported late.

Protection is a tracked state, not a promise (Rev 1.9a, V19-A06). Sending the stop with the order is a request invariant: an order without one is never sent. What happens after the fill is recorded as one of three states:

  • CONFIRMED_PROTECTED, when the broker read back the stop;
  • PROTECTION_UNCONFIRMED, while re-reading or re-attaching;
  • CONFIRMED_UNPROTECTED, which leads to an emergency reconcile, reduction or halt.

While protection is unconfirmed, the position's full exposure is shown as at risk and Attention is raised. VAN does not promise how many seconds this lasts when the broker or network fails. The current router does step 3 at once and trips the kill switch (F-031); whether Rev 1.2's sequence also replaces that for automated trades is an owner decision (OQ-07) — the trade-off is a few seconds' exposure versus a guaranteed spread cost and a halted account.

  • Size follows the stop. Quantity is derived from allowed currency risk, stop distance, contract/tick value, currency conversion and venue margin rules, rounded down to the venue step; below the venue minimum the answer is "too small to trade at this risk", never a rounded-up size.
  • The owner may tighten, never widen. The SL stepper and chart drag move the stop closer only. Widening is refused by protection.py and the pre-trade stop-integrity check.
  • Venues without broker stops (ZSE/VFEX, StopMode.SOFTWARE) are excluded from manual entry until the owner explicitly accepts software-stop risk for them.
  • Break-even and trailing moves from protection.py are optional per trade; they only tighten.

7.4.4 Optional take-profit

  • The owner may set a TP, leave it empty, or change or remove it later. A TP set at entry goes in the same order request as the stop (OrderCommand.targets). TP changes never add risk, so they use the lighter exit confirmation (§7.4.8).
  • Validation: on the correct side of entry, at least the venue's minimum distance; the sheet shows the R multiple ("TP is 1.8× your risk").
  • Where a venue cannot hold a TP, ProtectionManager holds it as a software exit and the screen says so.

7.4.5 Entry types

Market (MARKET_WITH_SLIPPAGE_CAP template) and limit or stop pending orders (LIMIT_AT_TOUCH and related sealed templates). A pending manual order expires (default end of session).

Pending orders and risk (Rev 1.9, AUD-02).

  • While resting: the order reserves its full worst-case risk, margin and exposure, including partial fills and sibling orders. New pending orders and modifications pass the current risk gate before transmission.
  • If the order becomes ineligible: VATI watches risk, news blackouts, mandate or mode changes and data freshness. When an order becomes ineligible, VATI tries to cancel or replace it before it fills, where the broker protocol allows.
  • If it fills anyway: a broker can execute a resting order before a cancellation arrives, so VAN never claims it can prevent a fill after the fact. On an unexpected fill, VAN keeps the broker's result as authoritative, reconciles family and portfolio exposure at once, applies authorised protection and risk-reduction controls, and raises Attention.
  • Unknown outcomes: never resent automatically.
  • Tests: a cancel racing a fill, partial fills, several outstanding orders, and a lost response.

7.4.6 Adding to positions

Every add joins the existing trade's position family (lifecycle/family.py): risk, exposure and the family stop are judged on the family as a whole, so three adds cannot become three separately "within limits" trades that are jointly over the limit.

Add to… How it works for the owner What governs it
A winning manual trade (OD-08, OD-23) Exactly like a new manual entry: same screen, same automatic stop inside the order, same optional TP, same review and biometric approval. Offered only while the position is winning: in profit on a fresh quote at the moment of approval. The manual risk profile (§7.4.2) applied to the family: the add's risk plus the family's existing open risk must stay within the family's original risk budget, the owner.manual budget and account limits. max_positions_per_instrument caps the number of adds. No adds to a losing position (no averaging down; martingale and unlimited_averaging_down remain hard-forbidden).
An automatic (VATI) trade (OD-10) From the trade's position screen: "Add to this trade". Same review sheet; the system shows which of the trade's rules allowed or refused the add. That trade's own risk profile: its strategy's risk budget (risk_budget_for(strategy_id)), its strategy's ScalePolicy (e.g. the position has already paid for itself, family healthy, time since last add, each add smaller than the last), its family risk envelope, preservation having nothing outstanding, and the account's temperament. And, as for manual trades (OD-10, OD-23): the trade must be winning, meaning in profit on a fresh quote at owner approval and again at broker submission. A quote that flips between the two refuses the add. The add is attributed to the strategy family with origin=OWNER_MANUAL. A strategy with no registered scale policy (NO_SCALING) cannot be added to manually either.

In both cases the add's stop is placed inside its order, and the family stop rule applies: a new add can never make the family's worst-case loss larger than its profile allows.

Anti-martingale tests (AUD-21):

  • A losing position offers no add, and a direct request is refused.
  • A position that turns losing between review and approval refuses the add at approval, and one that turns losing between approval and submission refuses it at submission; this applies to manual and automatic trades alike.
  • A direct API request to add to a losing automatic trade is refused.
  • A sequence of adds never takes family risk above its original budget.
  • Simultaneous fills of two adds are counted once against the family.
  • Concentration across currency legs is included.
  • A loss streak never raises the size of the next add. The automatic expansion engine (lifecycle/expansion.py) remains in its current SHADOW mode; owner-requested adds use the same scale-policy checks without waiting for that engine to go live.

7.4.7 Hand to VAN, and take back (OD-09)

7.4.12 Owner trading-behaviour diagnostics (OD-14, from the external audit §3.1)

7.4.13 Owner pattern extraction — turning winning habits into a candidate strategy (OD-14, audit §3.2)

VAN studies the owner's manual trades to find setups where the owner does well, and offers them as candidate strategies:

  1. Learn from all manual trades, winners and losers, using features actually known at entry (instrument, session, recent move and volatility, trend state, news context, and only a planned holding horizon recorded before entry; realized time held is outcome metadata, not an input). Learning only from winners — as the reference implementation does — cannot tell whether the same conditions also lose often, so it is not allowed.
  2. Express each pattern as an explicit, readable rule ("EURUSD buys in the London session after a pullback of 0.5–1× the normal day, trend up on H4") with how often it occurred and its result versus the owner's other trades.
  3. A pattern that beats the owner's average becomes a candidate strategy and goes through VATI's normal validation (deflated Sharpe, probability of backtest overfitting, walk-forward windows), then paper and shadow, then owner-signed promotion. It never trades directly from extraction.
  4. While it is a candidate, VAN can show "this setup matches your best past trades" on the chart and review sheet, as information only.

This keeps §7.4.10's rule intact: manual trades do not train or promote existing automated strategies; extraction creates a new, separately validated proposal.

7.4.14 Continuous governed strategy development — consolidated OD-37

The weekly strategy-development loop is a mandatory backend capability, BE-036, not a frontend add-on and not an independent trading runtime. It composes the existing VATI evolution, drift, backtest and validation machinery only after BE-035 has qualified the evaluator. The forecast addendum supplies richer target/data/model/calibration lifecycle capabilities through the same research coordinator.

Causal loop: durable schedule/run identity → complete trade/opportunity and market-cohort ledger → diagnostics and loss-cluster hypotheses → immutable candidate version with parent/protected-path classification → versioned dataset and chronological study → qualified execution-realistic evaluation → evidence registry → optional owner promotion proposal → exact signed stage transition → monitored deployment/demotion → outcome ledger. Every edge names its producer, consumer, authority, persistence and failure outcome.

Losses are grouped to generate hypotheses, never used alone to conclude a filter improves performance. Compare winners, exposure time, opportunity frequency, strategies and regimes. Input-distribution drift and loss of predictive information are separate measurements. Manual-trade pattern extraction creates new candidates and never silently contaminates an existing automated strategy's entry statistics.

A bounded standing research grant can authorize offline scheduling, hypothesis generation and evaluation; it does not approve a live candidate. Distinguish automatic computation of evidence from changes to an official admission/promotion state. If an existing owner contract requires a fresh signature for a particular stage, request it before that transition; do not silently infer it from a scheduled job. A live promotion binds exact candidate/model, evaluator, dataset, mandate, policy, account scope and qualifying evidence. Automatic demotion is allowed only as already admitted reduce-risk behaviour and is itself auditable.

The default weekly cadence is a scheduling policy, not a guarantee that every week produces a strategy. One run ID binds period, source data and evaluator; interrupted retries resume the same run. All variants and failures remain in an experiment ledger. Only qualified candidates are presented as promotion opportunities, but failures, model/data faults and deteriorating current-strategy health remain visible and may require Attention. A monthly report compares tried, qualified, rejected, promoted and demoted candidates, with costs and samples—not a list of only winners.

Evaluation contract: actual train/validation/test folds and frozen cutoffs; independent holdout accounting; all attempted variants; data and quote conventions; full costs; deployment-faithful account controls; genuine look-ahead refusal; and independent simulator oracles. Year buckets are not walk-forward windows. A test that deliberately supplies a future bar and changes the result is not a proof of leakage freedom. DSR/PBO are reported with their formula and cohort; neither is a generic chance that the strategy has skill. All model/calibration outputs remain evidence, not order or sizing authority.

Completion: BE-036 must be fully usable and reproducible even if no candidate qualifies economically. Lack of profitable models never justifies fabricating edge; it also does not excuse missing software capabilities. Economic admission is a separate exact-artifact gate. See VAN-CX-010–014 and the forecast-source contracts.

7.5 Positions and order history

Live cards display at-a-glance P&L/direction/SL/TP and protection, while tapping presents a native position sheet and then full detail. 7.8 Trading workstation architecture — applying the REA study (OD-15)

Source: research/VAN_REA_MT5_TRADINGVIEW_FULL_RESEARCH_20261015.md (static study of MetaTrader 5 500.6260 and TradingView 1.20.84.0.1002505 Android clients; neither app was run) and the review in research/VAN_REA_REVIEW_20261015.md. These are architecture lessons, not code or design imports: no competitor code, assets, layouts, styles or paywall logic are used, and VAN's locked visual design and authority model govern. Each item below has its own requirement (VAN-NX-040…047) and tests.

What VAN has today (checked in code, 15 October): each Android trading screen fetches its own data once per refresh tick through one-shot repository calls (TradingRepository.kt, LaunchedEffect(tick) in OverviewScreen.kt/PositionsScreen.kt); there is no shared reactive store and no de-duplication of subscriptions. The chart is a single Compose Canvas (TradeChartCanvas.kt) with pan/zoom/crosshair and price levels, drawn inside recomposition, with no separate renderer lifecycle and no indicator or drawing model. On the backend, VATI's ExecutionReceipt already distinguishes ACCEPTED, PARTIAL, FILLED, REJECTED, CANCELLED, EXPIRED and UNKNOWN; order entry types are MARKET | LIMIT | STOP | PASSIVE_LIMIT | CONTRACT_BUY with time-in-force DAY | GTC30.

7.8.1 One shared market and portfolio projection (REA-VAN-007, -011)

  • A single on-device store exposes quotes, bars, positions, orders, account and risk state as reactive streams (StateFlow), keyed by canonical instrument identity (venue, symbol, asset class, tick size, digits, contract size, quote currency) — never by ticker string alone.
  • Demand-driven subscriptions: screens declare what they show; the store subscribes once per instrument and stream, however many screens show it, and unsubscribes when nothing visible needs it. Fast price ticks are coalesced separately from slower portfolio, approval and strategy changes.
  • Freshness is data: every value carries source, event time, received time, revision and a stale/offline/unknown state. After a gap, the store reloads a revisioned snapshot from the gateway; cached values are shown as cached with their age.
  • Home, Trading Home, chart, positions, Quick Hub and Floating VAN all read this store, so they always agree on the same revision. Only the broker reconciler can assert that a position exists or is protected.
  • Replaces the current per-screen polling.

7.8.2 Two chart budgets, one chart model (REA-VAN-002, -003)

  • Native preview charts (sparklines and small candle previews) for Trading Home, watchlists, search results, potential-trade cards and position cards: cheap, no heavy chart engine per row.
  • Professional chart workstation for the instrument terminal, with its own renderer that owns frame production outside Compose recomposition: rendering pauses when hidden, survives surface or texture loss, keeps the viewport across rotation and process death, and has an accessible flat fallback. Compose owns the controls, sheets and account and security chrome around it.
  • A renderer-independent chart capability contract defines what the chart can do: chart types, intervals, symbol and layout, drawings, indicators, replay, alerts, news markers, trading overlays, zoom, undo/redo, multi-chart sync and theme. Any renderer (the existing native canvas, a new native engine, or a qualified web engine) implements the same contract, so a renderer can be swapped or degraded without rewriting navigation, research or trading. The renderer choice is made by measurement on the Samsung S24 (OQ-14), not fixed in advance.

7.8.3 Chart bridge rules (REA-VAN-004)

If any part of the chart runs in a web engine, messages between the app and the engine use a typed, versioned envelope (schema, session ID, message ID, sequence number, expiry, capability name, payload, data revision, authority_effect: NONE), with one allowed origin, size limits, capability allow-listing, deny-by-default, no arbitrary JavaScript, cancellation and teardown per session. The engine never receives broker sessions, owner tokens or VATI grants. Allowed outputs remain those in §14.3.

7.8.4 Indicators and drawings as saved models (REA-VAN-008)

  • Indicators are typed, versioned instances (type, parameters, pane, source timeframe, provenance tier PRESENTATION | VATI_SOURCE | RESEARCH), saved per instrument and workspace, with add, edit, reset-to-default and undo/redo.
  • Drawings (trend lines, levels, channels, Fibonacci, risk/reward boxes, notes) are durable records with exact price and time anchors, revision history and optional notes, not overlay pixels; they sync across layouts and devices and survive restarts.
  • Workspaces (layout, intervals, indicator set, drawings) are saved and restorable.

7.8.5 Replay sandbox (REA-VAN-009)

An isolated historical chart session that steps bar by bar through past data, for owner practice, reviewing past trades and testing ideas. Replay has no route to any order endpoint by construction; practice trades in replay are recorded separately and feed the owner's behaviour diagnostics (§7.4.12) as practice data, never as real results. Replay sessions are deterministic from the bar data and seed, so a review can be reproduced.

7.8.6 Complete order vocabulary (REA-VAN-005, -006)

  • Backend additions, venue by venue as each adapter supports them: stop-limit entries; fill policies (fill-or-kill, immediate-or-cancel, return) where the venue offers them; specified expiry in addition to day and good-till-cancelled; and a distinct broker-confirmed resting state. In code it is PLACED, and the screen label is Working (AUD-19). Today ACCEPTED covers it. Each venue adapter maps its raw states (MT5, cTrader, Deriv) to the canonical enum with tests, and an unknown raw state becomes OUTCOME_UNKNOWN, never success.
  • A per-venue capability matrix decides which order types, fill policies and expiries the app offers; the app never offers what the venue cannot do.
  • The app never collapses order states into one spinner or "success". The state names follow reference/VAN_STATE_MACHINES_REV1_9.json (historical model; proposed readiness delta in contracts/CRITICAL_STATE_AND_FINANCIAL_CLARIFICATIONS.md) (VAN-SM-TRADE_ORDER, extended from the pack's VNX-SM-TRADE_ORDER with race transitions): DRAFT, RISK_REVIEW, OWNER_REVIEW, AUTHORIZED, BROKER_PENDING, PLACED (working), PARTIAL, FILLED, MODIFY_PENDING, CANCEL_PENDING, CANCELLED, REJECTED, EXPIRED, OUTCOME_UNKNOWN. Each is shown distinctly, and an uncertain result is reconciled against the broker before any repeat is allowed. A stop request is not a cancellation, and a partial fill still carries exposure.

7.8.7 Chart observations feed VAN's research (REA-VAN-012)

With the owner's consent, the chart can send structured observations to the backend: instrument and timeframe under study, viewport and selected bars, indicator settings, drawn levels with exact anchors, the owner's notes, replay experiments, and why a potential trade was taken, dismissed or judged wrong. 7.9.4 Disconnecting and deleting

  • Disconnect stops trading on the account, revokes or destroys its credentials immediately and keeps all history.
  • Delete schedules archiving after a grace period (owner-selected duration under OQ-15; the parent's 7-day suggestion is not an approved default) with a "cancel deletion" banner, then archives. History is kept under the retention policy; credentials are destroyed at once.
  • Both are blocked while the account has open positions, working orders, unsettled or in-flight funds, unknown-outcome operations or live risk leases (OD-25). The owner must close, cancel or let them settle first. There is no "mark unmanaged" escape. The block is checked against fresh broker and VATI state; a stale "no positions" never passes. Pending OAuth attempts are discarded and strategies bound to the account are unassigned, each listed on the confirmation sheet.
  • Cancelling a deletion restores the record, its history and its link intent. It does not restore destroyed credentials, OAuth grants or broker entitlements. The account returns as RELINK_REQUIRED: re-authentication, broker readback and, where needed, A4 re-enrolment come before any trading. A cancellation never resurrects an account the broker has already closed.

7.9.5 Funding

VAN orchestrates funding inside the app (OD-18). Funding works in three parts:

  1. Deposits, withdrawals and transfers in VAN (§7.9.12). Each is offered only where a fresh capability check says the account supports it. It runs through the provider's verified API or its verified hosted flow, and counts as done only on broker readback. Where a provider supports neither, VAN shows why and links to the broker's cashier.
  2. Funding ledger: deposits, withdrawals, internal transfers, credits and bonuses, and fees are read from the broker's balance-operation history (MT5 balance and credit deals, cTrader cash-flow history, Deriv statement), reconciled, timestamped and shown per account. Unclassified operations are flagged for the owner to label.
  3. Flow-adjusted risk and performance (fixes F-033): drawdown is measured against a peak adjusted for external flows (a withdrawal lowers the peak by the same amount; a deposit raises it), daily loss excludes the day's net flows, and performance is reported time-weighted so deposits and withdrawals do not look like profits or losses. Broker credits and bonuses that cannot be withdrawn are excluded from risk capital by default. A withdrawal that would leave open positions under-margined is refused with its reason (AUD-05). The refusal is a server-side check against fresh provider margin, open positions, working orders, reserved funding intents and quote age. Pending or unknown collateral fails closed.

  • Withdrawal: DRAFT → BENEFICIARY_VERIFIED → QUOTED → AWAITING_A4 → AUTHORIZED → SUBMITTED → PROVIDER_PENDING → PAID.
  • Alternatives for both: REJECTED | FAILED | OUTCOME_UNKNOWN | REVERSED | EXPIRED.
  • Transfer: follows the deposit shape and needs both the debit and the credit read back.
  • SETTLED and PAID come only from broker or provider reconciliation, never from a redirect, an HTTP 200 or a model.

Effective-once business outcome, not a transport guarantee. Persist intent/outbox before send, use provider idempotency where supported and reconcile unknown outcomes. Every intent carries an idempotency key. A timeout after submission becomes OUTCOME_UNKNOWN and is reconciled by intent or transaction ID; it is never resent. A reversal is a new compensating event, and the original is never erased. Provider references survive process death.

Approval. The device-signed approval binds:

  • the exact action, account and provider;
  • the amount, fees and net;
  • the quote, the beneficiary digest and the expiry.

A changed quote needs re-approval, and the amount is immutable after approval. Beneficiaries are masked vault references. Card numbers, CVVs, bank secrets, OAuth codes and one-time passwords are never stored in Android or written to logs. A newly added beneficiary carries a safety delay before its first withdrawal.

  • stable internal account IDs;
  • regression tests for account_upsert and the existing signed OAuth flows;
  • capability revisions that expire;
  • verified provider web origins;
  • redaction of source data.

A method never appears enabled just because a button exists.

Rollout. One admitted provider rail first (OQ-19), in sandbox or demo, then failure and reversal reconciliation, then more providers only with evidence. No production provider credentials or live transfers are used during preparation.

Interaction with risk. Settled flows feed the funding ledger (part 2) and the flow-adjusted risk (part 3, F-033). Pending deposits are never counted as available collateral. A withdrawal that would leave open positions under-margined is blocked with its reason, not merely warned about.

7.10 Trading modes — Normal, Risky, Aggressive (OD-20; PR #92)

The owner wants trading modes. They are already designed and partly built on PR #92 (chatgpt/vati-opportunity-modes-20261008, head 923ecf9; design docs/VAN_VATI_OPPORTUNITY_TEMPERAMENT_MODES_REV1.md). This section adopts that design. It does not re-invent it.

What a mode changes. A mode changes the quality bar and the share of the already-signed per-strategy risk budget the system requests. It never raises the ceiling, never changes the mandate or its authority mode (OBSERVE … AUTONOMOUS_LIVE), and never enables adds or pyramiding.

Mode Behaviour (PR #92 starting values, not yet calibrated)
NORMAL Selective and consistent; requests 25–55 % of the signed strategy budget
RISKY Admits broader valid setups; requests 45–85 % of the budget; the ordinary Risk Authority still decides
AGGRESSIVE Highly selective: needs high market quality and independently admitted positive-edge statistics. Requests 70 % of the budget, or up to 100 % for exceptional evidence, never above it. Without evidence it does not trade. Caps early partial-take at 15 % of the remaining position.

Authority. A mode is set per account or per strategy in a new mandate version, carrying a second, content-bound owner signature (trading-temperament-admit). Any later change to the mandate invalidates that signature. There is no hot switching: a change needs a new signed mandate, a restart and reconciliation.

Every entry is still checked in full. Each entry still passes a sealed TradeIntent → Risk Authority → router check.

8.3 Analysis of each story

For every story cluster VAN records:

  • What it is: event class (from the EventRegistry taxonomy), entities, affected currencies, assets and instruments (declared exposure only), scheduled or unscheduled.
  • How surprising: for scheduled releases, the existing surprise score; for unscheduled news, a novelty grade — new, follow-up, correction, or already reflected in price (the market had moved before the story).
  • Which way it cuts for each trade: the existing concern per position and candidate (against longs, against shorts, both, unclear).
  • How reliable: source status (§8.2), contradictions between sources, whether the market's first reaction agrees with the story.
  • What history says: the event history profile (§8.4).
  • Uncertainty: stated, not hidden; "direction unclear" is a valid result.
  • Attention signals (OD-14, audit §3.4): deterministic detectors run on every refresh: a velocity spike (a topic's story count in the last hour well above the previous hour), triangulation (the same story from several independent outlets) and keyword surges. They feed story ranking, corroboration and the caution rules; on their own they never trigger a trade. Thresholds are calibrated on VAN's own feeds, and detectors that fire on publisher metadata rather than content are not allowed (the reference implementation removed one for exactly that reason).
  • Owner keyword watchlists: the owner adds keyword monitors (for example "RBZ", "ZiG", "gold export", a ZSE company name); matching stories are tagged, ranked up and can alert.

A classifier proposes event class, severity and concern for raw stories: deterministic rules first; a bounded language model may fill gaps. Every proposal records classified_by, a model-only classification is weighted below a rule-based one, and a model can never set source status, history numbers or any decision output.

  1. Headline episodes. Extend events/episodes.py to unscheduled news: the reaction is measured from the moment the story was first seen, at the same 1 min / 5 min / 30 min / 4 h horizons, in normal-range units, with MISSING marks kept. Scheduled-release episodes continue as now.
  2. Analogue search. Add event class, surprise or novelty bucket, source status and direction-of-surprise to the analogue feature vector (cognition/analogues.py), keeping retrieval deterministic and replayable.
  3. Event history profile. For the current story and instrument, from the closest analogues:
Field Meaning (owner wording)
sample_size, coverage, mean_distance "Based on 14 similar events since 2024" / "Too few similar events to rely on"
direction_split "EURUSD fell in 10 of 14"

For events with liquid prediction markets (§8.2), the profile also records the implied probability before the event and how far it moved, so a rate decision can be scored as a surprise against what the market expected, not only against economists' forecasts. Armed plans (§8.10.3) for such events may use it as their expectation, with the same two-source rule (Polymarket and Kalshi agreeing within a tolerance).

Thin evidence is shown honestly and limits automatic use: with EVIDENCE_THIN, only the default caution rules apply, never history-scaled ones.

8.5 How news changes trading decisions

News acts through these defined paths and no others. Each path is computed and logged from day one; it changes live trading only after it has run in shadow mode, been scored against what actually happened, and the owner has signed it live for that path (OQ-08). The owner sees a monthly "did news help" report per path: losses avoided, gains missed, sample size.

Path What changes Rule
D1 Size of new trades (auto and manual) New-trade size multiplier = the lower of the existing materiality multiplier and a history multiplier that shrinks size when adverse_p90 is large relative to the stop Never above 1; stops are never widened to fit; floor stays 0.25× except under D2
D2 Timing of new trades Defer new entries after a high-impact event until history's time_to_settle has passed, and during "coverage degraded" or "unexplained move" states Can extend but never shorten the calendar blackout; the calendar stays the blackout authority
D3 How orders are placed When history shows spread widening, choose passive or limit templates or DO_NOT_EXECUTE for the window Uses the existing sealed execution templates and their cancel_on_event_state
D4 Open trades before a scheduled high-impact event If history says a typical adverse move would eat most of the stop cushion and the trade is not yet protected (break-even or better), VAN-managed trades get a preservation action: partial close or move to break-even, per that trade's lifecycle policy Owner-managed (manual, not handed over) trades are never touched; the owner gets an alert with those options and the history behind them
D5 Open trades after adverse unscheduled news Corroborated or official adverse news moves the thesis to RISKIER or WEAKER; if trade health turns FAILING, preservation acts on VAN-managed trades; owner-managed trades get an alert SINGLE_SOURCE news alone cannot trigger an exit
D6 Ranking of potential trades Confidence lowered through the existing event-risk multiplier; candidates that conflict with corroborated adverse news are suppressed with the reason shown Confidence stays display/ranking only
D7 New trades created from news (made fast by the live bundle, §8.10) Patterns found in the episode library (for example, continuation after a large verified surprise) become event-driven strategies. They go through backtest, paper and shadow like any strategy and are promoted live only by owner signature; once live they generate potential trades that the Risk Authority sizes normally This is the only way news can lead VAN to open or enlarge a trade. A headline alone never does (unvalidated_research_to_live is forbidden).

My recommendation is to keep the rule that news can never directly increase risk (D1–D6 only reduce risk or change timing), and to let news create trades only through D7's validated strategies. Allowing a headline to raise size directly would bypass the evidence the strategy pipeline requires. Changing that is an owner decision (OQ-09).

Every brief answers, in this order and in everyday words:

  1. What happened — one sentence, sources named, status in words: Official, Confirmed by 3 sources, Single source — unconfirmed, Sources disagree, Corrected.
  2. Why it matters for you — which trades or instruments, and through what ("affects the euro side of EURUSD").
  3. What usually happens — the history profile in one or two lines ("In 14 similar releases EURUSD fell 10 times; typical move about 42 pips; usually settles within 25 minutes"), or "Too few similar events to rely on".
  4. What VAN is doing — the exact D1–D7 actions in force, or "nothing".
  5. What it means for your open trades — per position: "works against your EURUSD buy; in 3 of 14 similar cases the move was bigger than your stop", with the exit and protection buttons from §7.4.8.
  6. What would change this — next scheduled release, a confirmation or correction, or when the story stops counting.

8.9 Acceptance and negative tests

Two syndicated copies of one wire count as one source; a story seen only from one wire stays SINGLE_SOURCE and cannot trigger D4/D5; an official correction moves the cluster to CORRECTED and re-computes effects; a feed outage below the category minimum shows "coverage degraded" and triggers D2 only inside a high-impact window; an unexplained price jump raises caution until a story arrives; history with fewer than the minimum analogues reports EVIDENCE_THIN and D1 falls back to the default multiplier; a D1 multiplier is never above 1 and no stop is widened; D2 never shortens a calendar blackout; D4 never acts on an owner-managed trade; every D-path in SHADOW mode produces a logged "would have" record and no order; a path is live only with an owner-signed promotion record; a D7 strategy without promotion evidence cannot produce a live potential trade; a model rewrite that adds a price, instrument, count or direction is rejected; history counts in briefs match the episode records exactly; replaying the ledger reproduces the same profiles and effects; briefs never contain a forbidden word (lint); notification dedupe survives restart and multiple devices.

8.10.3 Scheduled releases: armed plans

For each high-impact release on the calendar, an event strategy prepares an armed plan before the release (default T−15 min), shown to the owner and frozen at T−2 min:

  • Instrument and direction mapping from the normaliser's declared "stronger" direction (e.g. US CPI above consensus → USD stronger → EURUSD sell).
  • Thresholds in surprise units from the event's own history (e.g. trade only if |surprise| ≥ 1.0 of its usual miss), and a no-trade band in the middle.
  • Stop and target from history: stop beyond the historical adverse move at the chosen percentile, target near the typical move, time exit at the history's time_to_settle — the stop and target travel inside the order.
  • Execution guards: maximum spread at entry (e.g. ≤ 3× normal), slippage cap (MARKET_WITH_SLIPPAGE_CAP or limit), maximum delay after release (e.g. no entry later than 20 s after the release time), quote freshness.
  • Size from the event strategy's own budget in the mandate (event.* strategies), reduced by drawdown tiers and the account's limits like any trade.

At the release second VAN fetches the official value, computes the surprise against consensus, and if every condition holds sends one order with its stop and target. Everything is decided before the release; nothing waits for a model.

Two sources must agree before entry (OD-13). At the release second VAN fetches the figure from the official issuer and from the second structured source (the paid calendar feed) in parallel. An armed plan enters only when both have delivered the figure and they agree (exact match after unit normalisation; a forecast-only or revised-previous mismatch does not block, an actual mismatch does). If the second source has not delivered within the plan's maximum entry delay, or the two disagree, the plan stands down for that release and records why. This follows the recorder's existing rule that one source is not a fact. The cost is a few seconds per release; the latency ledger measures exactly how many, per event class, and replay and paper trading (§8.10.8) use that measured wait so results reflect it.

8.10.4 Unscheduled headlines

  • Only headlines from the paid fast sources can trigger entries; free and model-classified headlines cannot.
  • A deterministic classifier matches headlines to whitelisted event classes that have a validated strategy (for example: unscheduled central-bank rate move, confirmed currency intervention, a named commodity supply shock). Each class has a declared direction map, thresholds and history profile.
  • Two fast, independent sources must agree (squawk + X, or squawk + official) before an unscheduled entry, with any proposed official-only exception requiring a separate explicit owner policy amendment; that exception is not admitted by this specification. Rumour wording ("sources say", "reportedly") never triggers an entry.
  • Language models are not on the entry path — too slow and not deterministic. They may write the brief afterwards.
  • Entries use smaller size than scheduled-release trades by default and the same stop-inside-order, spread and delay guards.

8.10.5 Owner live news trading

The owner can trade news manually through the same bundle:

  • News Live screen: live squawk and official headlines as they arrive (source, status, seconds since release), countdown to armed releases, the history card ("last 14 releases: EURUSD fell 10 times, typical 42 pips, settles in 25 min").
  • Armed manual ticket: before a release the owner prepares a ticket and signs it with biometrics in advance. The ticket covers the instrument, maximum risk, stop and TP from history, and which direction to take for an upside or downside surprise. At the release the owner needs one tap, with no biometric step.
  • The pre-signature is a typed, narrow grant (NewsArmedTicketGrant, Rev 1.9, AUD-04), bound to:
    • the account and the owner's device;
    • the exact event instance and event strategy;
    • the direction-rule digest and the maximum risk in exact minor units;
    • the stop-policy and limit-policy revisions (spread, slippage, quote age, price bounds);
    • the mandate digest;
    • the earliest-use and expiry times (e.g. T−10 min to T+60 s), and a one-use nonce.
  • What the tap can do: choose only within that envelope. The order goes out only if:
    • both sources have delivered the figure and agree (OD-24, as OD-13 requires for armed plans);
    • the current mandate, account state and VATI risk gate pass;
    • the nonce is unused.
  • What voids it: any change to the stop policy, the mandate or source health, a feed failure, quote drift or the window expiring voids the grant. No model, chart or bridge can sign one. Before any live use, an independent authority review and owner acceptance are required.
  • After the release, ordinary manual entries (§7.4) remain available, with the brief and history shown on the review sheet.

Rev 1.10 readiness clarification: SIGNED → ARMED requires verified signature, scope/mandate/policy revision, an allowed pre-arm window, account readiness, required feed health and unused nonce. It does not require future release actuals to be known. ARMED → USED additionally requires the event agreement, fresh risk/price and atomic intent/nonce claim. The stricter guard set is a proposed revision for owner and independent admission, not a silent rewrite of the parent signed lock. See contracts/CRITICAL_STATE_AND_FINANCIAL_CLARIFICATIONS.md.

8.10.6 Infrastructure and latency discipline

  • The bundle runs on the always-on trading server (van-trading-core), not on the phone; the phone shows and confirms.
  • Feed clients use websockets or streams where offered; official pages are fetched by a release-second scheduler with tight timeouts and no retries that could double-act.
  • Server clock disciplined (chrony/NTP); every hop timestamped: source publish time, received, parsed, decided, order sent, broker acknowledged, fill.
  • A latency ledger records those timestamps for every release and headline, per source. If a source's measured delay drifts beyond its threshold, it is downgraded from "release-grade" automatically and armed plans that depend on it disarm.
  • Server location relative to the broker's trade server is measured; moving the server closer is an operational decision, not assumed.

8.10.7 Risk limits specific to news trading

  • News trades belong to event.* strategies with their own budget in the signed mandate, well below the normal per-trade budget at first; a daily cap on news-trade losses; at most one news trade per instrument per event.
  • No entry if spread, quote age or delay limits are exceeded — the plan simply stands down and says why.
  • Feed disagreement, a value that fails sanity checks, or a broker reject on a news trade disarms that event class for the session.
  • Stops are inside the order; history-based stops are never widened after entry; time exits are mandatory.
  • All forbidden behaviours (martingale, stop removal, revenge risk, trading on stale data) apply unchanged.

8.10.8 Proving it before it trades live

  1. Replay each event strategy over history with realistic delays (the measured latency ledger, or conservative assumed delays before measurement exists), real spreads around releases (tick data), and slippage.
  2. Paper trade live with the real feeds for a minimum number of events per class, recording the full latency ledger and what would have happened.
  3. Shadow on the live account: decisions made and logged, no orders.
  4. Owner-signed promotion per event class, with a small live budget first, then reviewed monthly in the "did news help" report (§8.5).

An event class whose paper results do not survive realistic spreads and delays stays in shadow, however attractive the idea.

8.10.9 Bundle acceptance tests

Release value arriving before its scheduled time is rejected; official site lockup at release time stands the plan down without retries that double-act; surprise inside the no-trade band produces no order; spread above the cap at entry stands down with reason; entry later than the maximum delay is refused; a release with only one source delivered, or two sources disagreeing on the actual, produces no order and a recorded stand-down; the second source arriving after the plan's maximum entry delay produces no order; rumour-worded headline produces no entry; unscheduled headline from one fast source only produces no entry; stop and TP are inside the single order request; armed manual ticket used outside its window or with a changed parameter is refused; latency drift downgrades a source and disarms dependent plans; daily news-loss cap stops further news trades; replay with realistic spreads is reproducible from the ledger.

12.6 Keeping the new frontend clean (no pollution)

The new UI must look and behave like the locked design, not like the old app wearing new colours. Rules, enforced by the build rather than by review alone:

  1. Separate Gradle modules. :van-next:ui (screens, components, theme) depends only on :van-next:contracts (view-state types, ports, route IDs) and the admitted domain modules listed in VAN_NEXT_ADMITTED_DEPENDENCIES.json. Legacy presentation packages (command/* screens, trading/ui, design/VanTokens, design/components, overlay renderers, legacy aura renderers) are not on its classpath, so importing them fails compilation instead of relying on a lint warning.
  2. Adapters are the only bridge. Legacy functions reach the new UI through port adapters in :van-next:bridge, which may call legacy controllers and repositories but exports only :van-next:contracts types. Copy-pasting a legacy composable into the new module is rejected in review and caught by a duplicate-code check against the legacy UI packages.
  3. Token-only styling. Colours, dimensions, radii, elevation and motion come from the :van-next:ui design tokens; a lint rule rejects literal colours and dimensions in screens.
  4. One string catalogue built from §2.6. A lint rule rejects uncalibrated certainty wording (§2.6), "signal" and other banned trading phrases in UI strings. A positive test proves labelled market-implied likelihood and PBO text pass.
  5. No preview data in release. Preview fixtures live in a debug-only source set. A release-build check fails if any fixture or mock class, preview asset or fixture source set is present, checked by compiled source-set and asset provenance, not by searching UI strings. DEMO / NOT LIVE is a fixture watermark. Real demo and read-only account badges ("Demo", "Read-only") are legitimate production labels. A mutation test injects mock data and must fail the build; a positive test shows a real demo account badge renders.
  6. Admission record for exceptions. Any new dependency of :van-next:ui is added to the admitted-dependency file with a reason, owner, expiry and replacement plan; the file is hashed and the hash is checked in CI.
  7. Visual check. Screenshot tests compare each new screen with the owner-approved native goldens (§2.5) in both themes; "old hierarchy in new colours" fails VA8.

13. Data consistency, failures, caching and offline modes

13.1 Independent source domains

VAN displays aggregates over markets, orders/positions, DIAL tasks, Muse, Attention, voice and device state. These have different data ages and authority. A single global LIVE label must not hide stale positions or a disconnected task stream. Every panel carries source_id, observed_at, revision, optional valid_until, and confidence-of-observation distinct from probabilistic model confidence. The orb shows overall risk through deterministic precedence, while details expose each source's truth.

13.2 Event and snapshot reconciliation

Each source emits or polls a versioned snapshot and optionally ordered events. event_id deduplication is durable across process restarts within defined retention limits. Cursor gaps or source revision conflict cause a read-only fresh snapshot, not side-effect replay. For positions, order/cancel requests have stable client-generated intent identity and backend idempotency. For tasks, a newer authoritative FAILED event supersedes an earlier client RUNNING state, without a false celebratory progress animation. If source identity cannot be reconciled, render UNKNOWN and offer drilldown.

13.3 Cache policy

Cache display models and last-known quotes/positions only according to domain privacy and TTL; keep encryption for sensitive local data, provide lifecycle-aware invalidation on logout/device revocation. Cached data must show last verified update, source and age. The user can inspect historical charts, project snapshots and prior evidence offline, but cannot be told an unconfirmed action succeeded. A pending owner action awaiting reconfirmation is a distinct state from a queued readonly fetch.

14.1 Non-negotiable separation

14.2 Signed owner action lifecycle

             → bound device/A4 biometric challenge when needed

14.3 Trading order and chart boundary

The chart bridge is a bounded, untrusted display/interaction bridge. Allowed chart outputs include symbol/timeframe/drawing changes, InspectPosition, DraftTradeIntent (opens the §7.4 review sheet; carries direction, optional entry price, a tighter stop or a TP, never a size) and RequestExplanation; forbidden chart outputs include direct placeOrder, modifyOrder, closePosition, cancelOrder, raw setLeverage, changeRiskLimit and sendBrokerCommand. The native trading owner-intent layer handles all effectful operations with VATI and broker readback. Stop/target risk calculations remain owned by certified strategy/risk/lifecycle services, never a generated floating editor.

14.4 Device, privacy and recovery

Keep explicit pairing, revocation, secure storage, native biometric handling, signing and privacy mode. Sensitive absolute trading values must be masked in Home orb hub, Android notifications, floating overlay, watch/screen capture previews and position detail when policy requires it. Do not falsely guarantee screen-capture protection merely because the design includes a toggle; verify Android Secure Flag and approved sharing controls on physical device. Ensure no automated provider login or mic capture without supported owner permission. Rollback to old UI is possible without weakening release signing or downgrading sensitive stores.

2. Pending orders and protection

An order request includes its protective stop; that does not guarantee every venue accepted a stop or every later fill remains protected. Protection states are REQUESTED, BROKER_CONFIRMED and UNCONFIRMED/UNKNOWN, with source revision and exact position. Recovery re-reads, re-attaches where admitted, and invokes the authorized risk-reduction fallback at its deadline. Network failure prevents a success claim; a deadline schedules/escalates a bounded action rather than guaranteeing it succeeded within seconds.

Risk reservations cover resting and simultaneous sibling orders. Cancellation or modification can lose a race with a fill; the broker event wins and is reconciled. A take-back increments the management epoch and fences new automatic actions, but any already-sent action is reconciled rather than represented as magically cancelled. The final owner-visible status states which operations remain in flight.

3. Full monetary units and flow adjustment

Money, fees, collateral and executed quantities use integer minor units or declared decimal scale and currency/contract identity; conversions use authoritative timestamped rates and explicit rounding. Risk sizing includes contract value, price distance, quote/account conversion and venue steps—never a universal lots = currency risk / raw price-distance shortcut.

A deposit or withdrawal is an external cash flow, not P&L. Adopt a specified, tested flow-adjusted performance method. For a unitized baseline, a verified external flow at valuation NAV issues/redeems units at that NAV; trading return comes from NAV changes, not unit count. Alternatively use correctly timed subperiod returns. The accounting policy must define valuation ordering, simultaneous trade fills/fees, credits, reversals, transfers, negative or zero capital and unresolved flows. Synthetic examples are test fixtures, not account reports.

Removing cash should not fabricate a trading loss, but it can legitimately reduce collateral and increase exposure relative to remaining capital. Therefore tests distinguish flow-neutral performance from margin/risk eligibility: a withdrawal that would under-margin open exposure is refused; flow-adjusted historical drawdown does not grant spendable cash. Deposits never erase prior trading losses. Non-withdrawable credit is not risk capital by default. Unknown movements are quarantined for reconciliation, not guessed.

4. Effective-once effects under uncertainty

“Exactly once” is the intended business invariant, not an assertion that a network offers universal exactly-once delivery. Unsupported provider idempotency demands a verified reconciliation protocol. If outcome cannot be determined, keep OUTCOME_UNKNOWN and block duplicate risk rather than falsely report success.

Withdrawal approval binds beneficiary digest, currency, amount, fees/net, quote revision, expiry and owner/account identity. Server-side transactional reservations must serialize competing withdrawal/order usage. A callback redirect or HTTP200 is not provider settlement. Transfers require separate debit and credit confirmation and partial/unknown recovery.

30.7 Sellability and executable economics

Buy and sell checks are independent. Where supported, run read-only simulations against the stated chain/router/contract/pool state at the declared quantity, with caller assumptions, gas/fee conventions and finality recorded. Success proves only the stated simulation result at that state. It does not promise that a later sell will execute, survive changed taxes or obtain the same liquidity.

Separate three records:

  1. ExecutableQuoteFacts: observed side, quantity, currency, executable quoted cash/depth, provider/route and state anchor, availability/expiry and fee-inclusion components. No projected exit cash or fill probability is permitted in this facts record.
  2. ExecutionEconomicsForecast: modeled exit proceeds, execution/failure costs or fill probabilities, with explicit target, horizon, artifact/transform/calibration lineage and applicability. A probability requires its precise observable event; an amount forecast does not masquerade as a probability.
  3. NetEdgeAssessment: a derived economic calculation referencing the exact facts and forecast versions, uncertainty and cost convention. It identifies included costs to prevent double-counting.

For quantity q and horizon h, a cash-consistent research estimate is expected executable exit proceeds minus entry cash and any costs not already included. Compare it with the strategy's preregistered materiality/uncertainty floor and independent risk eligibility. Depth, slippage, transfer costs, gas and failure costs need not scale linearly with size. A small quote cannot be extrapolated into free liquidity at a larger quantity.
